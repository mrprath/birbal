*B2B fragrance venture - Market research, competitive intelligence, automation*

## The Vision

Commercial scenting is a growing market: hotels, offices, retail spaces all use ambient fragrance to shape customer experience. The market is dominated by a handful of players (ScentAir, Prolitec, Aroma360) with high margins and undifferentiated service. I see an opportunity to enter with better intelligence, better targeting, and a tech-first approach to customer acquisition.

## What I Have Built So Far

Before investing capital, I built the intelligence layer:

- Competitor website tracker: detects changes to pricing, products, and partnership pages across ScentAir, Prolitec, and Aroma360. Alerts on significant updates within hours.
- Trend monitor: tracks Google autocomplete patterns for fragrance-related queries to spot emerging consumer interest before it shows up in traditional market research.
- Social signal scanner: searches relevant forums and social platforms to capture market sentiment, pain points, and unmet needs.
- Structured analytics database: all signals stored in Supabase, tagged by event type and timestamped for trend analysis.

## The Approach

Most people entering a market start with a product. I started with information. Before committing to inventory, suppliers, or branding, I wanted to understand the competitive landscape better than the incumbents understand it themselves.

The deeper principle: intelligence is not about having more data. It is about having the right data, at the right time, with enough context to act on it.

## Starting a Business With Information, Not Inventory

Everyone I talk to about starting a fragrance business asks the same question first: what scents are you going to sell? It is the wrong first question.

### The Market Nobody Talks About

When people think of fragrance, they think of perfume bottles on department store shelves. That is the consumer market: crowded, brand-driven, and brutal on margins. The B2B market is different.

Hotels pipe ambient scent through their HVAC systems to create a signature atmosphere. Retail stores use it to increase dwell time: there is research showing that pleasant ambient scent can increase time spent in a store by 15-20%.

This is a market where the product is invisible by design. Customers do not choose their provider based on packaging or brand prestige. They choose based on reliability, customization, and whether the sales rep actually understands their space.

### What the Data Is Telling Me

After running the intelligence pipeline for several weeks, patterns have emerged:

- Competitor pricing pages change roughly monthly, usually small adjustments: they are testing price sensitivity
- Google autocomplete trends for commercial scenting terms are growing steadily, particularly in hospitality and wellness
- The most common complaint in online discussions is poor customer service and inflexible contracts
- There is almost no content marketing in this space. The incumbents rely on direct sales, which means organic search is wide open

### The Tech-First Advantage

I have a living, breathing intelligence layer that updates itself. When ScentAir changes their partnership page, I know the same day. When a new trend emerges in commercial scenting search queries, I see it within the week.

This is not about having better data. It is about having fresher data, structured in a way that supports decisions rather than just sitting in a report nobody reads.

> *The best time to understand a market is before you need to sell in it. The second best time is now.*
